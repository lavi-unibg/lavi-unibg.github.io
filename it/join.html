PhD Position — Reliable generative AI systems for high-stake decision making

Start: Spring 2027   |   Application timeline: Applications are expected to be open soon.

We invite expressions of interest for a fully funded Ph.D. position in “Reliable generative AI systems for high-stake decision making” at the University of Bergamo, Italy in collaboration with the Consiglio Nazionale delle Ricerche (CNR), Milan.

The project focuses on developing reliable, uncertainty-aware, robust, and computationally efficient AI systems, particularly generative models, multimodal models, vision-language-action models, and agentic systems, with potential applications in high-stake domains such as embodied AI, AI-assisted medical diagnosis, and time-series forecasting.

While the project focuses broadly on reliable AI, the exact research direction can be tailored to one or more of these areas, based on the candidate’s interests and background. Candidates with expertise in different areas of machine learning, computer vision, NLP, multimodal AI, robotics, agentic AI, or statistical learning are encouraged to apply.

Detailed project description

The increasing deployment of AI systems in real-world and high-stakes environments creates a need for models that can quantify their uncertainty, recognize their limitations, and make reliable decisions under changing or incomplete information. This is particularly important for modern generative, multimodal, and agentic systems, which may produce plausible but incorrect outputs, fail under distribution shifts, or take inappropriate actions with excessive confidence.

This Ph.D. project will investigate methods for developing reliable generative AI systems, with the goal of enabling robust, uncertainty-aware, and practically deployable models and agents. The research may consider modern day generative models, including vision-language models, vision-language-action models, diffusion models, agentic AI systems, and other foundation-model-based systems.

Research topics may include uncertainty quantification, calibration, out-of-distribution detection, uncertainty-aware reasoning and planning, selective prediction, abstention, verification, and safe fallback mechanisms. For agentic AI systems, possible topics include reliable tool use, confidence-aware routing and delegation, monitoring of multi-step trajectories, human escalation, error recovery, and uncertainty-aware decision-making in autonomous agents.

Possible applications include robotics, embodied AI, industrial inspection, medical AI, time-series forecasting, autonomous systems, and human-AI collaboration. The project will address both the theoretical foundations and practical deployment of reliable AI systems, including models operating under computational, data, and resource constraints.

The project lies at the intersection of trustworthy machine learning, deep learning, statistics, Bayesian methods, computer vision, natural language processing, multimodal learning, robotics, and efficient AI systems. Its aim is to advance AI systems that are not only accurate, but also capable of estimating uncertainty, adapting to changing conditions, recovering from errors, and requesting assistance when necessary.

Eligibility criteria

Essential qualifications:

  • Completed a Masters (or MSc) degree before the start of the program, from an accredited educational institution
  • A strong background in machine learning, statistics, mathematics, physics, computer science, engineering, or a related field
  • Experience with deep learning frameworks, such as PyTorch, and programming languages, such as Python or C++
  • Strong mathematical and analytical skills
  • Familiarity with modern deep learning architectures and methods
  • Strong interest in trustworthy, reliable, robust, and efficient AI systems
  • Excellent knowledge of English, both written and spoken

Desirable qualifications:

  • Hands-on experience with vision-language models, vision-language-action models, diffusion models, or tool-using AI systems
  • Experience with research projects, scientific software, or open-source contributions

Qualifications that are considered as plus:

  • Experience with uncertainty estimation, Bayesian machine learning, generative modelling, or calibration
  • Prior publication record in international peer-reviewed conferences or journals in machine learning, AI, computer vision, NLP, or robotics
  • Knowledge of conversational Italian

Research Environment

The successful candidate will be enrolled at the University of Bergamo (UniBG), in the Ph.D. Program in Manufacturing Ecosystems Enabled by Intelligent Technologies (MEET), and will carry out the research in collaboration with the STIIMA research unit of the National Research Council of Italy (CNR) in Milan. The student will also join the Joint Research Laboratory on Reliable Generative AI (ReGenAI), established by UniBG and CNR.

The project offers an interdisciplinary environment at the intersection of trustworthy AI, generative modeling, multimodal learning, uncertainty quantification, and agentic AI, with applications in robotics, autonomous systems, medical imaging, energy systems, and high-stakes decision making. The student will have exclusive access to GPU clusters of both UniBG and CNR and the possibility of applying to the Italian national GPU cluster for additional computational resources.

The Ph.D. research will be supervised by:

  • Develop a strong understanding of the relevant theoretical foundations, methods, and literature in the research area
  • Conduct independent, rigorous, and reproducible research under appropriate supervision
  • Publish research in leading international venues such as NeurIPS, ICLR, ICML, AAAI, IJCAI, ACL, EMNLP, CVPR, ICCV, ECCV or relevant journals in the field of AI and machine learning
  • Contribute to the development of open-source software or research tools related to the project
  • Work towards completing the doctoral thesis within the expected timeframe and meeting all institutional requirements

PhD Position — Reliable generative AI systems for high-stake decision making

Start: Autumn 2026   |   Application deadline: 16th June 2026. (11:59 AM Italian time)

We invite applications for a funded Ph.D. position in “Reliable generative AI systems for high-stake decision making” at the University of Bergamo, Italy, with a particular emphasis on “uncertainty quantification for small language models”. The project focuses on developing reliable and computationally efficient methods for confidence estimation, calibration, and uncertainty-aware decision-making in compact generative models deployed in edge and agentic AI settings.

Project Description:

The emergence of small language models (SLMs) [1] is enabling a new generation of efficient, deployable, and autonomous AI systems operating on edge devices and within agentic AI frameworks [2]. Yet, these models frequently exhibit unreliable confidence estimates, hallucinations, and poor calibration [3]. Current uncertainty quantification (UQ) approaches for large language models (LLMs) are often computationally prohibitive and insufficiently adapted to compact generative architectures.

This Ph.D. project will investigate uncertainty quantification for white-box SLMs (1 - 7B parameter models), with the goal of developing efficient, theoretically grounded, and practically deployable methods for estimating and utilizing uncertainty in generative AI systems. Research topics may include calibration under quantization, retrieval-augmented generation, epistemic uncertainty estimation, uncertainty-aware reasoning and routing, selective generation, and robust evaluation methodologies for generative models. Applications include edge AI, tool-using autonomous agents, and low-resource deployment scenarios where reliable confidence estimation is critical for safe and trustworthy operation (e.g., time-series forecasting, medical report generation).

The project lies at the intersection of trustworthy machine learning, natural language processing, Bayesian methods, and efficient generative AI systems, and aims to advance both the theoretical understanding and practical reliability of SLMs.

Bibliography:

  1. Subramanian, Shreyas, et al. "Small language models (slms) can still pack a punch: A survey." arXiv preprint arXiv:2501.05465 (2025).
  2. Belcak, Peter, et al. "Small language models are the future of agentic ai." arXiv preprint arXiv:2506.02153 (2025).
  3. Chuang, Yu-Neng, et al. "Confident or seek stronger: Exploring uncertainty-based on-device llm routing from benchmarking to generalization." arXiv preprint arXiv:2502.04428 (2025).
